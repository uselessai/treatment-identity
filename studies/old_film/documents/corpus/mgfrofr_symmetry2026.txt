                                         Abstract
                                          Old film restoration remains challenging because archival footage often combines
                                         scratches, blotches, flicker, blur, and temporal artifacts, disrupting the balance among
                                         structural recovery, regional texture repair, and fine artifact suppression. We propose
                                         Multi-Granularity Feature Decomposition with Ranking-Aware Regularization for Old
                                         Film Restoration (MgfrOFR), a multi-granularity extension of a Mamba-based old film
                                         restoration backbone. MgfrOFR first decomposes propagated features into coarse-to-fine
                                         restoration branches and feeds branch summaries back through channel-wise recalibration
                                         so different degradation scales can be represented without competing in a single feature
                                         stream. It then introduces a ranking-aware triplet regularizer that penalizes correlated
                                         temporal-similarity rankings across branches during training while preserving the baseline
                                         inference interface. Experiments on the Synthetic/Real-World Old Video (SRWOV) bench-
                                         mark show statistically significant fidelity gains on synthetic clips, favorable no-reference
                                         naturalness on real-world archival footage, and a bounded perceptual-quality trade-off.
                                         These results indicate that representation-level branch balance is an effective inductive bias
                                         for old film restoration under heterogeneous and asymmetric degradation.

                                         Keywords: old film restoration; multi-granularity feature decomposition; ranking-aware
                                         regularization; state space models; feature space symmetry

                      structural recovery and fine artifact suppression remain jointly encoded [3]. MambaOFR
                      introduced state space models to old film restoration, improving efficiency and establish-
                      ing the Synthetic/Real-World Old Video (SRWOV) benchmark [4–6]. Even so, it routes

                      share a common limitation: when heterogeneous signals compete in one latent space,
                      coarse recovery can overwrite fine evidence, and local artifact suppression can distort
                      global structure.
                            Here, symmetry operates at the representation level rather than as an object-level
                      shape constraint. A symmetric representation keeps restoration evidence balanced across
                      granularity-specific branches while maintaining consistent temporal correspondence across
                      frames. Because our branch-diversity objective is invariant under permutations of the
                      granularity branches (the symmetric group SG ), this balance is a permutation symmetry in
                      the machine-learning sense—a standard group-symmetry principle—rather than a visible
                      geometric one, placing our framework within symmetry-driven representation learning
                      for image restoration. This interpretation fits old film restoration because its degradation
                      space is hierarchical. Missing regions and large blotches require global context, grain and
                      flicker require regional modeling, and fine scratches or dust require local precision. Existing
                      multi-granularity designs mainly target detection or recognition [7–11]. Naive multi-branch
                      extensions can still collapse into correlated features unless branch diversity is explicitly
                      enforced. This gap motivates explicit granularity-separated restoration branches.
                            We propose Multi-Granularity Feature Decomposition with Ranking-Aware Regu-
                      larization for Old Film Restoration (MgfrOFR), which augments MambaOFR with two
                      complementary components (Figure 1). The Multi-Granularity Feature Extractor (MGFE)
                      reorganizes propagated Mamba features into coarse-to-fine restoration branches so that
                      structural completion, regional repair, and fine-detail suppression are carried by dedicated
                      descriptors instead of competing in one hidden stream. It also feeds branch summaries back
                      into the spatial stream through channel-wise recalibration. Ranking-Aware Triplet Reg-
                      ularization (RATR) complements this decomposition by discouraging redundant branch
                      behavior: it penalizes correlated rankings of temporal correspondences rather than impos-
                      ing identity-label supervision, making the regularizer suited to restoration.
                            Using a unified reproduction protocol on SRWOV, MgfrOFR preserves the efficient
                      Mamba backbone of MambaOFR while adding explicit granularity control at modest
                      overhead. The main contributions are summarized as follows:
                      1.    We formulate old film restoration through representation-level symmetry under het-
                            erogeneous degradation, treating coarse structural recovery, regional texture repair,
                            and fine artifact suppression as complementary feature components that remain
                            balanced despite spatial and temporal asymmetry.
                      2.    We design MGFE, a multi-granularity feature decomposition for Mamba-based old
                            film restoration that inserts learnable restoration tokens into propagated features,
                            aggregates them into coarse-to-fine branches, and feeds branch summaries back to
                            the spatial stream through channel-wise recalibration.
                      3.    We introduce RATR, a ranking-aware triplet regularizer that penalizes correlated
                            temporal-similarity rankings across granularity branches using differentiable rank
                            correlation, improving branch complementarity and reducing redundancy while
                            preserving the original baseline inference interface.
                      4.    We evaluate MgfrOFR on the SRWOV synthetic and real-world benchmark; statistical
                            tests, ablations, and complexity analysis show improved restoration fidelity and
                            no-reference naturalness at modest additional computational cost.

                      2. Related Work
                      2.1. Old Film and Video Restoration
                           Old film restoration differs from general video restoration because it must jointly
                      address structured artifacts (scratches, dust, blotches), photometric degradation (flicker,
                      grain, fading), and quality degradation (blur, noise). Traditional approaches decompose
                      the problem into detection and inpainting stages, relying on hand-crafted features with
                      interpolation-based filling [12] or sparse collaborative filtering [13,14]. Although com-
                      putationally efficient, these methods lack semantic understanding of video content and
                      leave photometric degradations largely unaddressed. Deep learning has shifted old film
                      restoration toward end-to-end frameworks: DeepRemaster [1] processes spatiotemporal
                      volumes with 3D convolutions, RTN [2] introduces bidirectional recurrent propagation with
                      a learnable soft defect mask, and RRTN [3] strengthens alignment via recursive transformer
                      networks with second-order grid propagation. MambaOFR [4] replaces computationally
                      expensive blocks with MambaIR-style [6] state-space modules and establishes the SRWOV
                      benchmark for dedicated old film restoration evaluation.
                           General video restoration methods such as video restoration with enhanced de-
                      formable convolutions (EDVR) [15], BasicVSR [16], BasicVSR++ [17], the Video Restoration
                      Transformer (VRT) [18], and the Recurrent Video Restoration Transformer (RVRT) [19]
                      provide strong temporal modeling, but they do not include defect handling tailored to
                      old film restoration. Recent diffusion and foundation-prior restoration systems, includ-
                      ing DiffBIR [20], SUPIR [21], and SeeSR [22], improve perceptual detail for blind image
                      restoration or real-world super-resolution, but they are not designed for temporal old-film
                      defects or SRWOV-style video restoration protocols. Despite this progress, existing old
                      film restoration methods still encode structural completion, regional repair, and fine-detail
                      suppression through a single propagated latent space. This motivates our multi-granularity
                      decomposition approach.

                      2.2. State Space Models for Vision
                            The computational cost of self-attention scales quadratically with sequence length,
                      posing a practical barrier for high-resolution video processing. Structured state space
                      sequence models [23] reformulate sequence modeling through continuous-time state transi-
                      tions, reducing complexity from O( L2 ) to O( L) while maintaining a global receptive field.
                      Mamba [5] further introduces selective state transitions with hardware-aware recurrence,
                      enabling content-dependent filtering. Adaptations of state space models (SSMs) to visual
                      domains have produced strong baselines: MambaIR [6] demonstrates 2D selective scan-
                      ning for image restoration, VMamba [24] builds hierarchical feature pyramids with four
                      scanning routes, and VSRM [25] alternates spatial and temporal Mamba blocks for video
                      super-resolution at linear cost.
                            These properties are well suited to old film restoration, which couples long temporal
                      dependencies, large spatial damage, and high-resolution input. MambaOFR [4] shows that
                      Mamba-based propagation can improve both efficiency and restoration quality relative
                      to transformer counterparts. However, linear-cost long-range aggregation alone does not
                      determine what restoration evidence each feature position retains; the backbone treats
                      all degradation scales uniformly. Our work augments the SSM feature flow with explicit
                      multi-granularity descriptors rather than replacing the backbone.

                      2.3. Multi-Scale and Multi-Branch Representations
                           A single feature scale often cannot encode global structure and fine-grained local
                      evidence simultaneously. Feature Pyramid Networks [7] and the High-Resolution Net-
                      work (HRNet) [8] show that explicit multi-scale decomposition improves dense prediction,
                      while MIRNet-v2 [26] shows that complementary multi-scale context enhances low-level
                      reconstruction quality. In person re-identification, the Part-based Convolutional Baseline
                      (PCB) [9] decomposes features along spatial stripes, the Multiple Granularity Network
                      (MGN) [10] combines global and local branches at different partition granularities, and ReI-
                      DMamba [11] adapts this paradigm to state space models. These methods demonstrate
                      that structured branches can expose complementary evidence, but their supervision and
                      feature semantics target recognition rather than restoration.

                      2.4. Diversity and Ranking Regularization
                           A consistent lesson across these works is that extra branches help only when they cap-
                      ture complementary cues rather than correlated copies of the same evidence. Contrastive
                      learning [27] provides a general diversity mechanism, but it does not directly regulate
                      pairwise ordering behavior across branches: two branches can produce different feature
                      magnitudes yet rank all candidates identically, yielding diversity in norm but redundancy
                      in discriminative structure. Differentiable Kendall’s Tau [28] enables gradient-based penal-
                      ization of correlated rankings, a property that naturally applies to flow-guided temporal
                      correspondences in old film restoration.

                      2.5. Representation Symmetry in Restoration
                           In artificial intelligence (AI)-based image processing, symmetry can be interpreted
                      beyond visible object geometry. A learned representation may be symmetric when comple-
                      mentary evidence sources remain balanced, comparable, and consistently organized across
                      samples; asymmetry appears when optimization favors one dominant cue. Feature-fusion
                      and correlation-based representation studies show that multi-view or multi-branch fea-
                      tures need explicit organization rather than simple concatenation [29,30], and feature-wise
                      modulation provides a practical way to inject conditioning signals into image-processing
                      networks [31]. For old film restoration, this view is useful because film damage is inherently

                      asymmetric across regions and frames. In MgfrOFR, this representation symmetry is made
                      precise as two group invariances of the training objective—branch-permutation invariance
                      under the symmetric group SG and direction-exchange invariance under the bidirectional
                      Z2 action (Section 3.7).
                            Our design is inspired by this line of work, but restoration changes the formulation
                      in three important ways. First, the branch tokens are inserted into propagated restora-
                      tion features rather than identity-oriented recognition embeddings. Second, the branch
                      summaries are fed back into the restoration stream through channel-wise recalibration,
                      so they affect reconstruction rather than only auxiliary supervision. Third, positives and
                      negatives are defined by temporal correspondence within and across clips rather than by
                      person identities. This discussion highlights two gaps in old film restoration: the absence of
                      explicit multi-granularity feature decomposition within the temporal propagation pipeline,
                      and the lack of diversity regularization that keeps parallel feature branches complemen-
                      tary. Unlike multi-scale pyramids, token/stripe recognition models, and generic diversity
                      losses, MgfrOFR is the only design considered here whose branch structure carries an
                      explicit permutation-and-exchange symmetry rather than acting on feature magnitude
                      alone. MgfrOFR addresses both gaps within a unified framework, as detailed in Section 3.

                      3. Methods
                      3.1. Problem Setup
                            Given a degraded old-film clip X = { xt }tT=1 , we aim to recover a clean clip Y = {yt }tT=1
                      with restored image content and reduced structured film defects and photometric degrada-
                      tion. Synthetic SRWOV clips provide paired clean frames for direct supervision. Real-world
                      archival clips have no ground truth, so we evaluate them with no-reference quality metrics
                      and qualitative inspection. Old-film degradations vary over both spatial scale and time,
                      and a restoration model must remove them without breaking temporal consistency.

                      3.2. Input Representation
                             Each input frame is a red–green–blue (RGB) image, and the baseline propagation
                      module converts the clip into backward and forward feature streams. At each time step
                                                                                          f
                      t, motion-aware propagation yields aligned feature maps f tb , f t ∈ RC f × H ×W . MgfrOFR
                      operates entirely on these propagated maps: MGFE takes each map, returns a recalibrated
                      map with the same spatial size and channel count, and produces the branch descriptors
                      used by RATR during training.
                             MgfrOFR retains the bidirectional propagation framework of MambaOFR [4] but
                      replaces its single-granularity restoration view with an explicit coarse-to-fine pipeline.
                      Given the degraded clip X = { xt }tT=1 , the baseline first propagates content backward and
                      forward so that neighboring frames are motion-aligned before restoration, and MgfrOFR
                      builds on these aligned features. Old-film damage is spatially and temporally asymmetric:
                      a scratch may cover one region in one frame and vanish in the next, yet restoration should
                      still balance coarse, medium, and fine evidence. This cross-granularity balance is the feature
                      space symmetry targeted by our design.
                             MGFE turns each propagated feature map into a token sequence, inserts learnable
                      restoration tokens, and reads branch descriptors from progressively fused token groups;
                      their summaries flow back to the propagated feature through channel-wise recalibration.
                      RATR acts only during training, where it discourages branches from inducing the same
                      temporal rankings, and is disabled at inference. The reconstruction head is inherited
                      unchanged from MambaOFR and outputs the restored frame. Figure 1 shows how these
                      components fit together.

                      3.5. Multi-Granularity Feature Extractor (MGFE)
                           Old films are damaged at very different scales: large missing regions, mid-scale
                      blotches, and thin scratches each require a different aggregation range. A single restoration
                      feature must serve all of them at once, so fine detail and coarse structure compete inside
                      the same channels. MGFE addresses this conflict by keeping the propagated feature as a
                      shared spatial carrier while exposing several restoration-token branches that aggregate at
                      different rates; the branch summaries are fed back to the carrier as a recalibration signal.
                      Each damage scale therefore receives a dedicated branch, while reconstruction still reads
                      from one common feature. Figure 2 details the design.

                      3.7. Symmetry Property of the Regularizer
                           The representation symmetry introduced in Section 1 has a concrete meaning in
                      MgfrOFR. It is not a geometric symmetry of the image content but an invariance of the
                      diversity objective under group actions that relabel branches or exchange propagation
                      directions. Two such invariances hold, and together they explain why the constraint is a
                      symmetry principle rather than a balancing heuristic, as discussed in Section 2.5.

                      Property 1 (Branch-permutation invariance). Both the intra term in Equation (23) and the
                      inter term Linter sum the differentiable Kendall statistic over unordered branch pairs g < g′ ,
                      and Equation (22) is symmetric in its arguments, DKTau( p, q; τ ) = DKTau(q, p; τ ). Permuting
                      the branch index of every descriptor by the same π therefore leaves the regularizer unchanged:

                      Property 2 (Direction-exchange invariance). The two propagation streams enter the regularizer

                            Symmetry versus balancing. These two invariances separate our constraint from a
                      tuned balancing term. Balancing targets an outcome(branches that end up contributing
                      comparably) and is usually enforced by an asymmetric, hand-weighted penalty on indi-
                      vidual branch magnitudes. RATR instead is built from group-invariant quantities (SG on
                      branches and Z2 on directions) and applies the identical constraint to every branch by
                      construction. Balanced, complementary branches follow as a consequence of optimizing
                      this symmetric objective, not as a separately weighted goal.
                            Scope of the claim. The symmetry here is functional and representation-level: an
                      invariance of the learning objective under permutation and exchange group actions, both
                      standard symmetry principles in machine learning, rather than a visible geometric symme-
                      try of the restored frames. We keep this reading deliberately narrow, and Section 2.5 relates
                      it to the broader use of symmetry in representation learning.

                      3.8. Training Objective
                           The generator objective combines the standard old film restoration losses used by the
                      MambaOFR baseline with our ranking-aware regularizer. We use an L1 pixel reconstruction
                      loss Lpix , whose robustness for image restoration is analyzed in [33], a Visual Geometry
                      Group (VGG)-based perceptual loss Lperc [34], an adversarial loss Lgan [35], and the ranking
                      regularizer Lratr :

                                            Ltotal = Lpix + λperc Lperc + λgan Lgan + λratr Lratr .                    (27)

                            Because MGFE only augments the propagated feature stream and RATR is a pure aux-
                      iliary term, Equation (27) fits into the baseline training loop without any other change. We
                      set λperc = 1.0 and λgan = 0.01, following RTN [2] and MambaOFR [4], and use λratr = 0.1,
                      λintra = 1.0, and λinter = 0.5 for the ranking term (see Section 4.5). The smaller weight
                      on Linter reflects the coarser ordering signal from clip-centroid negatives. Unless stated
                      otherwise, the full model uses feature dimension C f = 16, G = 3 branches, M = 12
                      restoration tokens, channel-wise average fusion in Equation (7), and differentiable Kendall
                      temperature τ = 0.1.

                      4. Experiments
                           We evaluate MgfrOFR under two complementary settings. Synthetic clips include
                      ground truth and therefore support full-reference fidelity and perceptual metrics. Real
                      archival footage lacks ground truth, so only no-reference naturalness metrics apply. We
                      keep these settings separate throughout this section because a single merged score would
                      mask the fidelity–naturalness trade-off that defines old film restoration.

                      4.1. Datasets and Evaluation Metrics
                      4.1.1. Datasets
                            We evaluate MgfrOFR on the SRWOV benchmark introduced with MambaOFR [4],
                      a dedicated old film restoration benchmark that provides both synthetic and real-world
                      test sets.
                            For training, we use the REalistic and Dynamic Scenes (REDS) training split [36], which
                      contains 240 video sequences, each with 100 frames at 720 × 1280 resolution. Clean REDS
                      frames are degraded on the fly with the synthetic degradation pipeline from MambaOFR,


                                                                                        https://doi.org/10.3390/sym1010000
Symmetry 2026, 1, 0                                                                                           13 of 28




                      which simulates scratches, dust, blotches, flicker, and grain and produces paired degraded
                      and clean training samples.
                           The synthetic test set contains 30 clips rendered with the same degradation pipeline
                      on held-out REDS sequences. Each clip has 100 frames at 720 × 1280 with paired ground
                      truth, enabling full-reference scoring. The real-world set contains 216 clips of archival
                      footage from the 1920s to the 1960s, with authentic mixed degradations and no ground
                      truth. We follow the original 216-clip SRWOV protocol [4]. It spans indoor portraits,
                      newsreel footage, conversations, and multi-person scenes and totals 29,256 frames for a
                      broad naturalness assessment.

                      4.1.2. Evaluation Metrics
                            Seven metrics cover three complementary dimensions. On synthetic data with
                      ground truth, we report peak signal-to-noise ratio (PSNR) and structural similarity index
                      (SSIM) [37] for pixel fidelity (higher is better), learned perceptual image patch similarity
                      (LPIPS) [38] for learned perceptual distance (lower is better), and deep image structure
                      and texture similarity (DISTS) [39] for joint structure-texture similarity (lower is better).
                      On real data without ground truth, we use natural image quality evaluator (NIQE) [40]
                      and blind/referenceless image spatial quality evaluator (BRISQUE) [41] for statistical
                      naturalness (lower is better), together with CLIP-based image quality assessment (CLIP-
                      IQA+) [42], which evaluates contrastive language–image pre-training (CLIP) [43] features
                      through learnable quality-aware prompts (higher is better). These groups probe pixel
                      fidelity, perceptual quality, and naturalness in turn.

                      4.2. Implementation Details
                      4.2.1. Training Details
                           We train MgfrOFR on the REDS training split [36] with the synthetic degradation
                      pipeline from Section 4.1.1. The optimizer is Adam [44] with β 1 = 0.9 and β 2 = 0.99,
                      an initial learning rate of 2 × 10−4 , and a MultiStep schedule with milestones at 50 K,
                      100 K, and 150 K iterations (γ = 0.5). Training runs for 200 K iterations on 4 NVIDIA
                      A100 graphics processing units (GPUs; NVIDIA Corporation, Santa Clara, CA, USA),
                      each with 80 GB of memory, one clip per GPU per mini-batch. Each sample is T = 7
                      consecutive frames cropped to 256 × 256. We use PyTorch 2.7 with Compute Unified
                      Device Architecture (CUDA) 12.4 and fix the random seed to 42. At inference, RATR is off
                      and frames are processed at full 720 × 1280 resolution without cropping.

                      4.2.2. Baseline Adaptation Protocol
                            The comparison reuses released implementations and published settings in one unified
                      environment. For clarity, we make each baseline’s adaptation explicit along four axes.
                            Retraining and computational budget. Dedicated old film restoration methods (RTN,
                      RRTN, MambaOFR) are retrained under the same REDS-based synthetic film degradation
                      pipeline as MgfrOFR. In particular, MambaOFR—our primary baseline—and MgfrOFR
                      are trained with an identical 200 K-iteration schedule on the same A100-class hardware, so
                      their computational budget for old film restoration adaptation is matched; RTN and RRTN
                      are reproduced under the same degradation pipeline with a comparable training budget.
                            Hyper-parameter tuning. We retain each baseline’s published architecture-specific
                      hyper-parameters rather than performing exhaustive per-baseline re-tuning. Re-tuning
                      every method to convergence on old film restoration would be computationally prohibitive
                      and could bias the comparison toward whichever baseline received the most tuning effort.
                            Degradation adaptation. Generic video restoration methods (VRT, RVRT, ShiftNet)
                      keep their published architecture and default optimization recipe; we replace only the
                      task-specific degradation generation so that they can be trained on old film restoration


                                                                                   https://doi.org/10.3390/sym1010000
Symmetry 2026, 1, 0                                                                                                14 of 28




                      inputs, and we report them as faithfully reproduced references whose training budget
                      follows each released recipe.
                           Reference systems. Enhancement-oriented methods (DeOldify, OldPhoto, DeepRe-
                      master) are not designed for paired old film restoration and are not retrained on the old
                      film restoration pipeline; we include them strictly as reference systems to contextualize
                      perceptual trade-offs, not as task-matched old film restoration baselines.
                           The comparison is therefore a unified reproduction study under a shared degradation
                      protocol, with a budget-matched tier for dedicated old film restoration methods. It does
                      not claim that every baseline has been exhaustively re-optimized for old film restoration.

                      4.7. Analysis and Limitations
                      4.7.1. Comparison with the Closest Prior Work
                           MgfrOFR is built directly on top of MambaOFR [4], so its contribution should be
                      distinguished from the baseline backbone. We do not claim originality for bidirectional
                      Mamba propagation, the degradation-aware prompt block, or the original reconstruction
                      head. The contribution lies in how propagated features are reorganized and regularized
                      before reconstruction.
                           Compared with MambaOFR, MgfrOFR differs in four aspects. First, the propagated
                      feature is no longer treated as a single restoration carrier; MGFE exposes explicit coarse-
                      to-fine branches. Second, these branches are summarized and fed back through channel-
                      wise recalibration rather than being used only for auxiliary supervision. Third, branch
                      diversity is constrained by ranking correlation on temporal correspondences, rather than
                      by an identity-based objective inherited from recognition tasks. Fourth, RATR is used
                      only during training, so the deployment interface remains compatible with the baseline
                      inference pipeline.
                           The key value of this design is representation-level symmetry. MgfrOFR does not make
                      object-level geometric regularity its target; instead, it examines how an AI restoration model
                      can maintain balanced coarse-to-fine feature spaces under asymmetric film degradation.
                      MGFE separates evidence by restoration granularity, while RATR keeps temporal-similarity
                      rankings from becoming redundant across branches. This provides a concrete symmetry-
                      related mechanism without overstating the mathematical scope of the method.

                      4.7.2. MGFE–RATR Synergy for Branch Balance
                         The ablation study indicates that MGFE and RATR play different roles in the full model.
                      MGFE creates the coarse-to-fine branch structure and provides channel-wise recalibration,



                                                                                         https://doi.org/10.3390/sym1010000
Symmetry 2026, 1, 0                                                                                             21 of 28




                      while RATR acts as a diversity regularizer that makes the branches more complementary
                      (Table 3). In the RATR-only configuration, MGFE branch descriptors are still computed
                      and used as multi-granularity ranking targets; only the channel-wise scale/shift feedback
                      is disabled. Thus, MGFE supplies the branch structure needed for coarse-to-fine feature
                      balance, whereas RATR suppresses redundant temporal-ranking behavior.
                            The asymmetric ablation behavior in Table 3 shows that MGFE recalibration alone
                      improves the baseline but does not reach the fidelity–perception balance of the full model.
                      This is consistent with the perception–distortion trade-off [48], which establishes that opti-
                      mizing a distortion measure such as PSNR does not by itself guarantee the best perceptual
                      behavior. The channel-wise recalibration in Equation (14) is conditioned on an averaged
                      branch context and is driven mainly by the pixel reconstruction loss. Without a diversity
                      constraint, the granularity branches can converge toward similar low-frequency corrections,
                      so the MGFE-only variant remains less effective than the full model on LPIPS and DISTS.
                      RATR mitigates this effect by acting on branch-ranking structure rather than on pixel error.
                      Penalizing correlated temporal rankings across branches (Equations (23)–(25)) prevents the
                      branches from collapsing onto a single fidelity-oriented direction and preserves comple-
                      mentary fine-grained evidence. The full model, therefore, gives the strongest combined
                      fidelity and full-reference perceptual result in this protocol.
                            The real-world results are consistent with this interpretation but do not prove content fi-
                      delity without ground truth. On the SRWOV real-world test set (Table 2), MgfrOFR achieves
                      the best NIQE (5.12) and improves BRISQUE relative to MambaOFR (24.79 vs. 29.79), while
                      RTN keeps the best BRISQUE score and MgfrOFR’s CLIP-IQA+ (0.4092) is lower than
                      OldPhoto, MambaOFR, and RTN. This pattern suggests that the multi-granularity decom-
                      position transfers useful restoration structure beyond the synthetic training distribution,
                      but the no-reference gains should be interpreted as evidence of statistical naturalness rather
                      than definitive perceptual superiority.

                      4.7.4. When Does MgfrOFR Struggle?
                           MgfrOFR’s advantage is not uniform across all degradation conditions. The ablation
                      and sensitivity results indicate that the full model is most useful when heterogeneous
                      degradation is present, such as structural damage co-occurring with fine scratches in the
                      same frame. On clips dominated by a single degradation type, such as uniform flicker
                      without spatial damage, the multi-granularity decomposition can add representational
                      overhead without proportional benefit, and the baseline or RATR-only variant may match
                      the full model’s fidelity.
                           The real-world CLIP-IQA+ result highlights a trade-off between naturalness and
                      enhancement-oriented perceptual quality. MgfrOFR’s CLIP-IQA+ (0.4092) is below Old-
                      Photo (0.4503), MambaOFR (0.4351), and RTN (0.4163), while MgfrOFR achieves the best
                      NIQE (5.12) and improves BRISQUE relative to MambaOFR (24.79 vs. 29.79). This pattern
                      reflects the fact that different no-reference metrics measure distinct aspects of restoration
                      quality. CLIP-IQA+ is trained on human quality annotations that correlate with sharp tex-
                      tures and high-contrast details [42], favoring methods that enhance perceptual sharpness
                      through learned global priors. NIQE and BRISQUE instead measure statistical deviation
                      from natural image distributions and penalize artifacts introduced by texture hallucination.
                      MgfrOFR’s granularity-based recalibration operates conservatively on the propagated
                      feature stream, which appears to preserve statistical naturalness while providing less
                      texture enhancement than the stronger CLIP-IQA+ baselines. For archival restoration,
                      NIQE/BRISQUE may be more relevant proxies when content fidelity is prioritized, but hu-
                      man evaluation is needed to validate that preference.

                      4.7.5. Limitations, Threats to Validity, and Future Work
                      Threats to Validity
                           Five threats affect the interpretation of the results. Dataset threat: all training uses
                      synthetic degradation applied to REDS clips, which introduces a synthetic-to-real domain
                      gap and may limit generalization to archival footage with correlated degradation chains not
                      modeled by the pipeline. Metric threat: evaluation relies on PSNR/SSIM/LPIPS/DISTS
                      for synthetic full-reference assessment and no-reference metrics for real-world assessment;
                      these metrics emphasize different aspects of quality and may not fully align with human
                      perceptual judgments. Human-evaluation threat: the study does not include an expert
                      reader study with film-restoration practitioners or archivists, so the practical value of
                      improvements on authentic footage is inferred from proxy metrics and qualitative inspec-



                                                                                   https://doi.org/10.3390/sym1010000
Symmetry 2026, 1, 0                                                                                              24 of 28




                      tion. Baseline threat: three baselines (VRT, RVRT, ShiftNet) required adaptation from video
                      restoration to old-film-specific degradation, which preserves their architectures but may
                      not reflect their optimal performance under a different training regime. Synthetic-to-real
                      threat: the real-world evaluation lacks ground truth, so improvements in NIQE/BRISQUE
                      reflect statistical naturalness rather than verified content fidelity, and the absence of external
                      validation on additional datasets limits the generalization claim.

                      Limitations and Future Work
                           RATR adds training-time cost because it requires branch-wise similarity matrices over
                      the batch-time descriptor set N̄ = Nb T. Its dominant cost therefore grows approximately
                      quadratically in N̄. Longer clips or larger effective batch-time products would make
                      this quadratic term more pronounced, so gradient checkpointing or selective ranking
                      construction, such as sampling a subset of anchors per iteration, will be needed for larger
                      training settings. The second limitation is the synthetic-to-real domain gap caused by
                      REDS-based training degradation. Although the real-world NIQE/BRISQUE results are
                      encouraging, authentic archival footage can contain correlated degradation chains that
                      synthetic pipelines do not model. The third limitation is evaluation scope: validation
                      is limited to SRWOV, and additional archival datasets plus human evaluation would
                      strengthen claims about generalization.
                           Promising extensions include adaptive branch activation conditioned on degrada-
                      tion severity, frequency-aware branch specialization that assigns structural recovery and
                      texture restoration to dedicated branches, and joint old film restoration and colorization
                      frameworks for archival digitization and cultural heritage restoration workflows [12].

                      5. Conclusions
                           We presented MgfrOFR, a Mamba-based old film restoration framework that augments
                      MambaOFR with explicit multi-granularity feature balancing. The Multi-Granularity
                      Feature Extractor (MGFE) reorganizes propagated features into coarse-to-fine restoration-
                      token branches and recalibrates the spatial stream through channel-wise feedback, while
                      Ranking-Aware Triplet Regularization (RATR) discourages branch collapse by penalizing
                      correlated temporal-similarity rankings during training.
                           With the final average-fusion configuration on the synthetic benchmark, MgfrOFR
                      achieves the strongest full-reference scores among the tested baselines, improving PSNR by
                      1.02 dB over MambaOFR (27.86 dB vs. 26.84 dB). On 216 real-world archival clips, MgfrOFR
                      obtains the lowest NIQE (5.12), improves BRISQUE over MambaOFR (24.79 vs. 29.79),
                      and shows a lower CLIP-IQA+ value (0.4092) than the sharper enhancement-oriented
                      references and old film restoration baselines. Because the real-world setting lacks ground
                      truth, these no-reference gains should be interpreted as evidence of improved statistical
                      naturalness rather than definitive proof of perceptual superiority. The ablation study further
                      shows that MGFE and RATR are complementary: MGFE alone improves the baseline but
                      remains weaker than the full model on full-reference perceptual metrics, whereas the full
                      model gives the best combined result when branch diversity is explicitly enforced through
                      RATR and average fusion.
                           These results suggest that representation-level branch balance is a useful inductive bias
                      for heterogeneous-degradation video restoration and practical archival digitization work-
                      flows. The main implication is that feature space symmetry and temporally coordinated
                      ranking behavior can improve restoration under degradation-induced asymmetry without
                      changing the baseline inference interface. Several limitations remain: the method inherits
                      the synthetic-to-real domain gap of REDS-based training; RATR adds a training-time cost
                      whose dominant batch-time ranking term grows approximately quadratically with the



                                                                                      https://doi.org/10.3390/sym1010000
Symmetry 2026, 1, 0                                                                                                     25 of 28




                      descriptor set; and evaluation is limited to the SRWOV benchmark and no-reference proxies
                      on authentic footage. Future work will focus on adaptive branch activation conditioned
                      on degradation severity, frequency-aware branch specialization for structural recovery
                      and texture restoration, lighter ranking constructions such as selective anchor sampling,
                      human-in-the-loop evaluation, and broader validation on additional archival benchmarks.
